About

I am a Senior Research Fellow in Statistics at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, with research interests spanning Combinatorics, Game Theory, Optimization, Number Theory, and Geometry. My work brings together the theory of stochastic games and modern high-dimensional statistics.

Alongside my research, teaching is central to how I work. I have served as a teaching assistant for a range of courses at the Indian Statistical Institute, including Design of Experiments, Probability Theory, and the Statistical Methods, and I have mentored many students preparing for the ISI, CMI, and olympiad examinations. I find genuine satisfaction in guiding students from a first encounter with a problem to a complete and rigorous solution, and much of the material collected on this site grew directly out of that teaching. My aim throughout is to make advanced mathematics approachable without diluting its rigour, and to help students develop the habits of careful reasoning that competition mathematics rewards.

Away from mathematics, I write in Bengali; my short-story collection Giraffe-er Dorakata Muffler was published by Sristisukh Prakashan in 2019.

Research

On a Switching Control Stochastic Game in Continuous Action Space

Stochastic Games · Optimal Control

A two-player zero-sum switching control stochastic game with two states and a continuous action space, framed through the metaphor of two competing political parties whose actions encode impartiality and cooperation. The ruling party controls the state transition at each stage. The work investigates the existence of non-stationary optimal strategies — Markov, semi-Markov, and order-two Markov types — under both discounted and limiting-average payoff criteria, and asks whether non-stationary strategies can outperform stationary ones.

A Mathematical Model and Skill Measure for Prediction-Market Games

Game Theory · Stochastic Processes

A formal stochastic-dynamic-game model for prediction-market play built on a background random process, illustrated throughout with a cricket match. The framework features a dynamic set of players who join and leave over time, an order book with first-in-first-out matching, and payoff functions tied to correctly forecasting the outcome. By deriving the structure of the optimal strategy, the work argues that effective play demands genuine skill — anticipating the underlying process and the behaviour of other players — rather than chance.

Determining the Latent Dimension of Dynamic Network Factor Models

High-Dimensional Statistics · Random Matrix Theory

A principled methodology for estimating the number of latent factors in networks that evolve over time. The approach combines dynamic matrix factor modelling with spectral methods from random matrix theory, using the lag-one covariance operator to suppress idiosyncratic noise and isolate the signal subspace. Drawing on the Marchenko–Pastur law, the spiked covariance model, and the BBP phase transition, the paper develops consistent rank estimators, establishes their theoretical guarantees, and illustrates the methodology on dynamic financial correlation networks.
